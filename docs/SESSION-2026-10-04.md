## 1. Social buttons in the header

Sam wanted the social buttons beside the wordmark bigger ("at least as tall as the logo
itself, they aren't really obvious") and Facebook added alongside Instagram and YouTube.

`SocialLinks` already took `only` (filter and order platforms). It now also takes
`size`, where `"header"` is the compact variant used in the top bar. The glyph is sized
as a fraction of the button (`h-3/5 w-3/5`) rather than a fixed pixel value, so it
tracks whatever the button does.

**Final size: 32px button, 19px glyph**, stepping down to 28px below 350px wide. Logo
height (40px) was tried first and Sam found it too chunky, so the settled rule is *not*
"match the logo" but "smaller than the logo, bigger mark than the 16px original". If
this gets revisited, that is the constraint to hold.

Order in the bar is Instagram, YouTube, Facebook. The footer, contact and about rows are
untouched and still use the default size.

## 2. Two layout faults the measuring turned up

Neither was visible on a phone, which is why they had survived.

**The logo was being distorted.** It is an `<img>` with `w-auto h-9` inside a flex row.
Flex shrank it horizontally while its height stayed pinned, so the wordmark rendered
142px wide naturally, 120px at a 360px viewport, 80px at 320px. Squashed, not scaled.
It is `shrink-0` now. **Anything added beside the logo has to be sized to fit, because
the logo will no longer absorb the squeeze.** The logo and the buttons step down
together on narrow phones (`h-7` / `h-8` from 350px) so the row still fits.

**The nav was overlapping the logo on tablets.** The full desktop nav needs about 790px
but was shown from `md` (768px). The result was nav text printed over the wordmark and
labels wrapping to three lines, from roughly 768px to 1150px. It switches at `lg` now,
so 768 to 1023 gets the compact menu.

## 3. Who It's For left the header nav

Sam asked whether merging pages would hurt SEO, specifically folding Who It's For into
another page, or putting Guides and FAQ into Who It's For or About.

Decided from his own analytics (90 days to 2026-10-04, admin excluded, 1132 views):

| page | views | from search |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | 471 | 39 |
| `/portfolio` | 227 | 11 |
| `/services` | 94 | 7 |
| `/pricing` | 74 | 6 |
| `/contact` | 64 | 12 |
| `/guides` | 51 | 3 |
| `/who-its-for` | 49 | 5 |
| `/about` | 47 | 9 |
| each individual guide | 2 to 7 | about 1 |

**What was done:** Who It's For was removed from the header nav only. The page, its
footer link and its sitemap entry are all intact, so nothing is deindexed. It earned 5
search visits in a quarter and had no contextual inbound links, only header, footer and
sitemap, so the header slot was the expensive part rather than the page.

**What was advised against:** folding Guides and FAQ into About or Who It's For. Each
guide targets its own long-tail query, and collapsing five URLs into one permanently
gives up four of those targets. The `/guides` hub is also what internally links them.
They are producing almost nothing today, but they are two and a half months old and are
the slowest asset to mature, so low numbers now are not the signal to cut them.

**If Who It's For is ever merged for real**, the move is into `/services` as a section,
with a 308 redirect (same pattern as `how-many-prototype-copies` in `next.config.mjs`),
not a delete.

Side benefit: one fewer nav link freed enough width that the social row no longer has to
hide on desktop. It had needed a hide band between `lg` and 1160px; that is gone and the
row now shows from 320px up.

## 4. The sideways scroll was the footer, not the carousel

The home page scrolled about 63px sideways between roughly 760px and 1000px wide. The
carousel looked guilty in a first pass and was not: its off-screen slides are clipped by
`overflow-hidden` and never expand the document. **Comparing bounding rects to the
viewport surfaces harmless clipped children.** The scan that actually found it looks for
elements where `scrollWidth > clientWidth` while `overflowX` is `visible`.

Two causes in the footer newsletter form, stacked, so fixing the first only revealed the
second:

1. **The email field could not shrink.** The footer goes to three columns at `md`, making
   each about 219px, which is exactly where `sm:flex-row` put the field and the Subscribe
   button side by side. A flex item defaults to `min-width: auto`, so the input would not
   go below its intrinsic ~200px and pushed the button 63px off screen. It stays stacked
   until `lg` now, and the field has `min-w-0`.
2. **Turnstile "normal" is a hard 300px box** and cannot fit a 219px column. The footer
   widget uses `"compact"` (150x140) now. `TurnstileWidget` takes a `size` prop for this;
   the contact form keeps `"normal"`, which has the room.

Footer column widths, for anything else that goes in there: ~219px at 768, ~299px at
1024, ~341px at 1152 and above.

## How this was verified

`playwright-core` is already in `node_modules`. It has no bundled browser, so drive the
system Chrome:

```js
chromium.launch({ executablePath: "C:/Program Files/Google/Chrome/Application/chrome.exe" })
```

Traps worth knowing: Next dev never reaches `networkidle`, so use `domcontentloaded`
plus a wait. Scripts must live in a relative `.tmp` inside the project or `require`
cannot resolve `playwright-core`. Tailwind class selectors like `ul.md:flex` are invalid
CSS in `querySelector` and need escaping.

Header clearance was measured at 320, 360, 375, 390, 412, 768, 900, 1024, 1160, 1280 and
1440, and the whole page swept for overflow at the same widths. Everything positive, no
overflow anywhere, confirmed against the live site after deploy.
